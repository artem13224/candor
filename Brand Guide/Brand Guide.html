Section 01 — Identity

One mark.
Many surfaces.

The Candor mark is a topographic landform — a small island read from above, contours peeling back to a single verified peak. Quiet at small sizes, distinct at large ones, never “the leaf.”

A short

Warm oat ground, forest‑black ink, a single committed green. Auxiliary tones earn their place — one border, one hover, one muted error. Nothing else dilutes the system.

Section 04 — Voice

Five rules
for writing.

The single fastest way to lose Candor’s voice is to write like a sustainability firm. Read these once before you put anything in a client’s inbox.

01
Say the thing.

The hardest sentence is the first one. Get to the point in the subject line, the opening line, and the first 12 words.

YES"Your B Corp score came back at 74. We need 80."
NO "Following up to circle back on the assessment outcomes."
02
Use small words.

One Anglo‑Saxon verb beats three Latinate nouns. "Help" not "facilitate." "Plan" not "strategic roadmap." If a word has more than three syllables, you usually have a shorter one.

YES"We help small brands get certified without the firm tax."
NO "We facilitate stakeholder alignment toward credentialing outcomes."
03
Be the person, not the firm.

First person singular. Sign the email, take the call, own the mistake. Candor never hides behind a "we." When you must use "we," mean it — "you and I."

YES"I read the audit notes last night. Two things worth talking about."
NO "Our team has reviewed the documentation provided."
04
Tell the uncomfortable truth.

If a client isn’t ready, say so. If a certification isn’t right for them, say so. The brand is built on being the one consultant who didn’t bill you for a project that wouldn’t have worked anyway.

YES"Honest answer: B Corp isn’t the right credential for you yet."
NO "There are exciting pathways to explore for your sustainability journey."
05
Don’t do the sustainability voice.

No "journey," "ecosystem," "stewardship," "impact narrative," "doing the work." No leaves in emoji. No earnest verbs of motion. Talk about emissions, audits, scope, fees, and timelines — the things being measured.

YES"Scope 1 and 2 measured. Scope 3 starts next quarter."
NO "We’re passionate about your impact journey toward a better tomorrow."
